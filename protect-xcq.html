XCQ: rebuilding a 2005 search tool

XCQ, the Inter-County Query, lets DA staff search people and cases across all Wisconsin counties. It was built in 2005, with no design system, inconsistent layouts, slow results and no accessibility support. I redesigned it on a new Figma design system, built from IBM's Carbon kit with light and dark modes, and validated it with stakeholders before engineering handoff.

Legacy XCQ · 2005
  • No design system or reusable components
  • Different layouts for search, results and detail pages
  • No column sorting
  • Every result loaded at once, so large searches were slow
  • Failed contrast and keyboard checks
  • Fixed width, hard to use on smaller screens
  • No documentation, so every update started from scratch
Redesigned XCQ · 2024
  • Design system from IBM Carbon: tokens, type scale and components, in light and dark
  • Consistent layouts built from shared components
  • Sortable columns with clear indicators and keyboard support
  • Pagination with a choice of 10, 25 or 50 per page
  • WCAG AA contrast, proper table markup, keyboard navigation
  • Layout adapts to monitor size and browser zoom
  • The Figma library as the single source of truth
Discovery

Understanding a tool people relied on daily

I ran discovery sessions with stakeholders, the business analyst and DA staff who use inter-county queries every day, and reviewed customer feedback reports for recurring problems. Direct access to end users was limited, so I also ran proxy sessions where analysts used the prototype in context while I observed.

Process

System first, then screens

Discovery

Sessions with stakeholders, the BA and DA staff; feedback reports; pain points mapped to business goals and technical limits.

Design system

Started from IBM's Carbon Figma kit: tokens, type scale, buttons, inputs, tables, badges and spacing, restyled with the PROTECT brand in light and dark modes.

Sketching

Rough layouts in Draw.io. Three directions explored; a single-page search beat a tabbed one for lower mental effort.

Prototype & refine

Interactive Figma prototypes reviewed by stakeholders, then two more rounds of refinement before handoff.

Challenges & trade-offs

Where the hard parts were

Modern CSS vs. older supported browsers

OptionDesign only for modern browsers
ChosenProgressive enhancement, with tested fallbacks

Some advanced CSS wasn't available in the supported browser versions, so the design had to degrade gracefully.

All results vs. pagination

OptionKeep loading every record at once
ChosenPagination with 10, 25 or 50 per page

Paging solved performance without forcing people to change how they scan results.

Brand new look vs. PROTECT continuity

OptionA standalone visual style
ChosenUse the PROTECT navy, gold and type

Staff move between the desktop app and XCQ, so the two should feel like one product.

Formal testing vs. proxy testing

OptionWait for direct access to users
ChosenObserve analysts using the prototype in context

Institutional limits blocked direct sessions. Proxy observation kept the work moving, and the decision tracker recorded what still needed confirming.

Reflection

Alignment was harder than aesthetics

“Applying patterns to our XCQ design is one of your real strengths.”
Jenn MacainagProject Lead, PROTECT
  • Legacy systems hide requirements that only appear in design review, so plan for iteration.
  • A design system is the most useful first deliverable in a legacy redesign; it creates agreement before any screen exists.
  • For legal staff, density, accuracy and speed matter more than minimalism.
