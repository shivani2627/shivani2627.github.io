AI in my workflow

Where AI speeds me up, and where I keep it out

On the Missouri DESE project I used Claude, Figma Make, Claude Design and Claude Code to deliver in six months. They shorten the slow, mechanical parts of the work. Decisions, research conclusions and accessibility sign-off stay with me.

Claude

Sorting raw notes

After stakeholder sessions I ask Claude to group my typed notes into themes. Then I go back to the source notes and check every theme before it reaches a slide.

ClaudeFigJam

Alternative IA trees

Once a card sort is done, I have it draft two or three navigation trees from the results. They're a starting point I check against real tasks, not the answer.

Figma Make

Clickable prototypes sooner

I turn approved wireframes into responsive, clickable prototypes so stakeholders can try real flows in review instead of reading static frames.

ClaudeHTML/CSS

Front-end fixes

I pair with Claude on small responsive layout fixes, then hand engineers a clear diff to review instead of a vague bug note.

My rules

No real student, district or case data goes into an AI tool; prompts use sample data only. I read every AI-generated line before it ships. Any project where AI meaningfully shaped the work carries an AI-assisted tag on this site.

How I work

Same discipline, every engagement

Most of my projects have had one designer, me. So the process has to hold up without a team around it: decisions are written down, the system comes first, and engineers are in the room early.

Understand the work

Stakeholder sessions, reviewing the current tool with business analysts, and watching people use it whenever access allows.

Audit before drawing

A heuristic review of what exists, so the redesign doesn't rebuild the same structural problems in nicer colors.

Structure first

User flows, roles, and IA in FigJam. Card sorts when navigation is the problem.

System, then screens

Tokens, variables with light and dark modes, components and variants in Figma before high fidelity, so 50 screens stay consistent.

Hand off and stay

Annotated specs and Dev Mode, then build reviews against the design until it ships.

Reviews

I run regular prototype reviews with stakeholders, BAs and developers, and update the design after each round.

Testing

When I can't reach end users, I run sessions with the people who support them and write down what still needs checking with real users.

Handoff

I walk developers through the file and the design document, and keep a decision log so settled choices don't get reopened.

About

I came to design through front-end code.

I started at Atos Syntel building FedEx pages as a UI developer and kept asking why screens were built the way they were. That curiosity turned into UX research, wireframes and, eventually, owning the full design of statewide systems.

Code still shapes how I design. I know what's cheap and what's expensive to build, I write specs engineers can act on, and I can open a stylesheet myself when a fix is small.
