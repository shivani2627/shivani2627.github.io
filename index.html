How I work

Same discipline, every project

On most projects I have been the only designer, so the process has to hold up on its own: decisions are written down, the system comes first, and engineers are in the room early. I also run workshops and design reviews, guide developers through builds, and mentor teammates on UX and Figma.

Understand the work

Stakeholder sessions, walkthroughs of the current tool with business analysts, and testing with real users or the people who support them.

Audit before drawing

A heuristic review first, so the redesign doesn't rebuild the same problems in nicer colors.

System, then screens

Tokens, variables and components in Figma before high fidelity, so hundreds of screens stay consistent.

Hand off and stay

Specs in Dev Mode, a written decision log, and build reviews with developers until it ships.

Behind this page

Yes, this portfolio has its own design system: 14 color tokens, three modes and a few shared components. It’s tokens all the way down. Take the two-minute tour →

Where AI fits

I use Claude, Figma Make and Claude Code for the slow, mechanical parts: sorting interview notes, first drafts of navigation trees, clickable prototypes and small front-end fixes. Research conclusions, design decisions and accessibility sign-off stay with me, and no real user data goes into an AI tool.

About

I came to design through front-end code.

I started at Atos Syntel building FedEx pages as a UI developer and kept asking why screens were built the way they were. That curiosity turned into UX research, wireframes and, eventually, owning the full design of statewide systems.

Code still shapes how I design. I know what's cheap and what's expensive to build, I write specs engineers can act on, and I can open a stylesheet myself when a fix is small.

Outside work, I sing, I cook, and I love being in nature. I won the Best Creative Dish award at Dallas StarChef, and I'm a mom to a 1-year-old.
